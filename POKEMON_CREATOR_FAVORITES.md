# PokeTuber Picks With Upgrades

## Purpose

Track Pokemon selected for the V2 PokeTuber Picks With Upgrades section. The goal is to feature roughly 5% of Pokemon, normally with two meaningful picks per creator. One Pokemon may carry a creator's section when it is an unmistakable channel icon and supports a feature-depth interaction.

- Josh watches each creator and nominates two Pokemon with a meaningful connection.
- The association and canonical Pokemon mechanics are checked before selection.
- This document records only the creator's channel, the chosen Pokemon, and the interactions to build. Research links, timestamps, and evidence notes stay out of the concept brief.

Each pick receives a bespoke upgrade such as a shiny reveal, transformation, evolution, special form, ability demonstration, habitat change, or creator-specific interaction. Picks are creator associations, not claims that every Pokemon is the creator's definitive favorite.

The section follows the spirit of Karen's advice: spotlight Pokemon people connect with, not only Legendaries, special forms, or the strongest battlers. An upgrade may reveal personality, ecology, an ability, or a memorable story instead of increasing power.

Each showcase links directly to the creator's YouTube channel as a free editorial spotlight. Present the section as fan-curated and unofficial; do not imply sponsorship, participation, approval, or endorsement by the featured creators.

Assign creator-associated Pokemon by species debut generation, even when an interaction also features mechanics from later generations.

### Curator Picks

- Appears after both creator sections and contains six of Josh's personal selections.
- Type spread: the current four share no types, covering Fighting/Steel, Rock/Dark, Psychic, and Ice/Bug across seven distinct types. Mega Lucario and Mega Tyranitar preserve their base typings.
- First pick: Lucario, connecting its Gen 4 debut and Mega Evolution with Sean Schemmel's English performance in *Lucario and the Mystery of Mew* and his voice work in *Yu-Gi-Oh!*.
- Second pick: Tyranitar, a non-Dragon pseudo-legendary built around Sand Stream and Mega Evolution. Its entrance engulfs the scene in a sandstorm, exposes the Rock-type special-defense boost within the storm, and then intensifies both the armor and weather as Tyranitar Mega Evolves.
- Mechanics note: Tyranitar and Mega Tyranitar are Rock/Dark types with Sand Stream. Rock-type Pokemon receive increased Special Defense during a sandstorm.
- Third pick: Reuniclus, representing Gen 5 through its unusual cellular design and Josh's extensive use of it during a Gen 5 playthrough. The battle space folds into Trick Room, the turn-order display reverses, and slow Reuniclus moves first as a special sweeper before an understated blue shiny reveal.
- Mechanics note: Trick Room reverses the usual move order within each priority bracket for five turns, allowing slower Pokemon such as Reuniclus to act before faster opponents.
- Fourth pick: Frosmoth, representing Gen 8 through the unexpected appeal of an icy moth, its distinctive Bug/Ice typing, and its elegant frozen-wing design. In a moonlit snowfield, incoming special attacks fracture across its scales and lose half their force before the wings release a glittering frost pattern across the scene.
- Mechanics note: Frosmoth's hidden ability Ice Scales halves damage received from special moves. Bug/Ice is shared only by Snom and Frosmoth.

- Interaction: Shadow Tag closes a dark psychic boundary around the opposing side and disables its escape controls. - Interaction: Start with Kantonian Exeggutor, then pull the camera dramatically upward as it changes into its towering Alolan form. Each form has a Regular/Shiny toggle. - Mechanics note: Harvest can restore a consumed Berry at the end of a turn and is guaranteed to activate in harsh sunlight.

## Showcase Systems

### Curated Shiny Showcase

- Shiny interactions appear only for selected creator-associated Pokemon, not every species.
- Each interaction should reflect the Pokemon's design or creator story instead of using one universal color toggle.
- Initial coverage target: roughly 5% of Pokemon, or about 50-55 species.

### Dynamax and Gigantamax Showcase

- Desktop and tablet: Dynamax and Gigantamax presentations appear side by side when space allows.
- Mobile: presentations stack vertically.
- Dynamax uses reusable type-based Max Move treatments.
- Gigantamax uses species-specific transformations centered on each signature G-Max move.
- Type-driven defaults may be overridden by species-specific interactions.
- All motion-heavy showcases require a reduced-motion alternative.
